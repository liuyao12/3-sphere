THE SEAM IN S³

Why exactly 100?

Start with one regular decagon running along a great circle of the 600-cell. The tetrahedra touching its ten vertices form a solid torus of 150 cells. Its boundary is a torus tiled by 100 triangles—each triangle belongs to one of 100 boundary tetrahedra.

This is the clean version of the claim: the torus is the boundary between two regions, not a generic smooth slice through only 100 tetrahedra. The smooth surface shown above passes through the same 50 boundary vertices; the faceted layer shows the actual 600-cell seam.

For the dual 120-cell, the count changes. The decomposition becomes two linked solid tori of 60 dodecahedra each. Their common boundary is a bumpy torus made from 200 regular pentagons. Turn on “120-cell seam” to see those pentagonal cycles projected into ℝ³.

01

Stereographic projection

A point (x, y, z, w) on S³ maps to (x, y, z)/(1 − w). One pole escapes to infinity; circles remain circles or become lines.

02

Hopf fibers

S³ decomposes into circles. After projection, any two distinct fibers are linked—even when the perspective makes them appear to pass apart.

03

The 600-cell

Its tetrahedra are regular in ℝ⁴. Projection preserves incidence and angles locally, but not lengths, so they stretch dramatically in ℝ³.

Built as a geometric explainer. The model generates the 600-cell from coordinates and reconstructs all 600 tetrahedra from edge cliques.
